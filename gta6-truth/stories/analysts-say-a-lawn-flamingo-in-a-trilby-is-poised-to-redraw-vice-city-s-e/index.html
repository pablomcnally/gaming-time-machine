Analysts have spent the morning staring at a lawn flamingo in a trilby and, after extensive charting and at least one biscuit, declared it a likely catalyst for social change.

Their theory is simple: once a flamingo adopts headwear, the whole neighbourhood watch model shifts from ‘concerned’ to ‘impossibly coordinated’.

The bird, which is not a real bird in any legal sense, is said to signal a broader movement toward decorative authority, where gardens become governance and sheds become think tanks.

This, naturally, will affect everything from driveway etiquette to who gets to stand near the barbecue without looking suspicious.

The analysts conclude that Vice City is not merely witnessing a fashion statement, but the birth of what they are calling ‘hard landscaping politics’.
